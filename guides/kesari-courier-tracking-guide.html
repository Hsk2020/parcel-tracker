Kesari Courier Tracking: AWB, Docket and GC Numbers

Updated 9 November 2026 · Hrudai Store Kart

Kesari Courier is a Gujarat-based courier and logistics company headquartered in Surat, with around 300 offices and coverage across more than 26,000 pin codes. It handles parcels and documents rather than the marketplace last-mile work that Ekart and Shadowfax do, so its tracking behaves more like a traditional courier.

What your tracking number is called

This is where most people get stuck. Kesari's booking receipt may label the same number in several ways, and all of them are the same code:

  • AWB number - air waybill, the term most couriers use
  • Docket number - common on transport and cargo receipts
  • GC number - goods consignment
  • Consignment number or receipt number

If a tracking box asks for an AWB or docket number, enter whatever is printed on your Kesari receipt. They are interchangeable.

Where to find it

  • The booking receipt printed at the branch when the parcel was handed over
  • The SMS sent to the registered mobile number after booking
  • The email confirmation, if booked through a business account
  • The sender's paperwork, if you are the recipient

If you are receiving the parcel and nobody has given you the number, ask the sender. They hold the receipt, and with most Indian couriers the sender is the only party the company will discuss the consignment with.

How to track

The official route is kesaricourier.com. The tracking field sits on the site, and entering the AWB, docket or GC number returns the current status and location history, pulled from Kesari's own system.

Kesari numbers are alphanumeric - letters and digits mixed - and typically 10 to 15 characters. If your number is all digits, check the receipt again; a missing letter is the most common reason a lookup returns nothing.

Why a new booking shows no record

Tracking records usually activate within a few hours of the parcel being picked up and scanned. Book in the morning and check in the evening, not ten minutes later. If nothing appears after 24 hours, the parcel may not have been scanned at the branch - which happens when a booking is entered but the physical parcel is still waiting for the daily dispatch.

When tracking fails, call

Some consignments never appear online: manual bookings, branch transfers, and anything where the barcode was damaged. In those cases the branch can find the parcel from the receipt details. Have ready: the tracking number, booking date, sender name, recipient name and the origin city. With those five facts a branch can locate a consignment that the website cannot.

Kesari's support is reachable on +91 93279 37653 and by email at support@kesaricourier.in. Their head office is in Surat, with a further office in Jaipur.

What to expect from the scans

Kesari's network is branch-based, so scans happen when a parcel arrives at or leaves a branch. On a long route you may see nothing for a day or more between two branches. That gap is normal - it means the parcel is on a vehicle, not that it has been lost. Five working days without any scan is the point at which it is worth calling.

Kesari against the national couriers

Kesari's strength is reach: around 300 branches covering more than 26,000 pin codes, which puts it in the same bracket as Gati and VRL for getting to district towns. The trade-off is the same as theirs - branch-based scans, so the timeline is coarser than a national courier that scans at every handover.

For a business shipping to a tier-3 town, that reach is often the difference between a delivered parcel and an RTO. For a metro to metro document, a national courier will be faster and will show more detail along the way.

What to check at the counter

  • Confirm the destination branch rather than just the city - a parcel addressed to a district town can sit at the district hub if the branch name is missing.
  • Give the recipient's mobile number. Branch-based networks call ahead, and a wrong number is the most common cause of a failed delivery.
  • Keep the booking receipt until delivery. It carries the number and the declared value.
  • Ask whether the service is door delivery or godown. Many transport-style bookings are collect-from-branch, and that surprises recipients.

Reading a stalled timeline

Look at the last branch named. If it is the origin branch and nothing follows, the parcel is waiting for dispatch. If it is an intermediate branch and stops there, the parcel is sitting and a call to that branch resolves it faster than the central helpline. If it reached the destination branch and stopped, the parcel is awaiting delivery or collection - and that is a local problem, not a transit problem.
