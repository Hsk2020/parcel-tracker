Blue Dart Tracking: Waybill Numbers and What They Show

Updated 9 November 2026 · Hrudai Store Kart

Blue Dart is the premium end of Indian express logistics - faster transit and higher rates than the mass-market couriers, and part of the DHL group. That changes what you should expect from its tracking: fewer scans, but each one closer to real time.

The waybill number

Blue Dart calls its tracking reference a waybill number, and it is normally eleven digits, all numeric. You will find it on the consignment note handed over at pickup, on the shipping label, and in the dispatch email if a seller booked it for you.

Blue Dart also issues a separate reference for the customer's own order, which is not a tracking number. If a number you were given does not work, check whether it is the seller's order ID rather than the waybill.

Where to track

The official route is bluedart.com - enter the waybill in the tracking box. Blue Dart also publishes a customer service line and responds to email, but for a specific consignment the tracking page answers faster than any phone queue.

What the scans tell you

Blue Dart's network is hub-based and air-heavy, so the pattern usually looks like this: picked up, moved to the origin hub, flown or driven to the destination city, arrived at the destination facility, out for delivery, delivered.

Because fewer hands touch a Blue Dart parcel than a surface consignment, there are fewer scans overall. A gap of a day between two scans is normal and does not mean the parcel has stopped moving.

Time-definite products

Part of Blue Dart's offer is a committed delivery time rather than a best-effort estimate. If you booked a time-definite product and it arrived late, the booking document - not the tracking page - is what a claim is judged against. Keep it.

If the parcel is late

  • Check the destination facility scan. If it shows "arrived", the delay is local delivery, not transit.
  • Ask the sender to raise it. The booking belongs to their account, and Blue Dart answers an account holder faster than a recipient.
  • For anything valuable, put the complaint in writing with the waybill number. A phone call leaves no record.

Proof of delivery

Blue Dart records who accepted a delivered consignment and when. If the status says delivered and you have nothing, ask for the POD - it names the recipient and settles most disputes without an argument.

Why Blue Dart numbers are short

An eleven-digit waybill is compact because Blue Dart allocates numbers centrally rather than encoding branch or service information into them. You cannot read the origin, the product or the destination out of the number - all of that comes from the scan history.

Air versus surface

Blue Dart runs both. An air consignment typically moves overnight between metros and produces two or three scans; a surface consignment on a long route can take several days and produces a similar number of scans, spread further apart. If your timeline looks sparse but the dates are progressing, the parcel is moving normally.

Declared value and claims

Blue Dart's liability on an undeclared consignment is capped. For anything valuable, declare the value at booking and keep the consignment note. A claim without a declared value usually recovers little more than the freight charge.

Tracking multiple consignments

If you ship regularly, keep waybill numbers in one list rather than reopening the tracking page for each. A tracker that stores your parcels lets you see which ones moved since your last visit instead of checking each number by hand.

Reading a sparse timeline correctly

Because Blue Dart moves consignments in fewer, larger steps, three scans can cover an entire journey from Chennai to Delhi. If you are used to a marketplace courier that produces eight entries for the same distance, a Blue Dart timeline can look incomplete. It is not - each scan simply represents a bigger jump.

The one scan worth watching is the arrival at the destination facility. Once that appears, the remaining variable is local delivery, and a delay from that point is a city-level problem rather than a transit problem.

The one habit that saves arguments

Photograph the consignment note before the parcel leaves your hands. It carries the waybill number, the declared value and the service level you paid for - the three facts every claim depends on. When a parcel goes missing three weeks later, that photograph is the difference between a straightforward claim and a dispute about what was actually booked.
