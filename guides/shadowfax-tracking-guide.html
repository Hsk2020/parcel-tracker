Shadowfax Tracking: AWB Format and What the Scans Mean

Updated 9 November 2026 · Hrudai Store Kart

Shadowfax grew out of last-mile delivery for marketplaces, and its tracking reflects that: frequent scans close to delivery, and thinner detail on the long middle of the journey.

The AWB format

Shadowfax airway bill numbers begin with SF followed by digits - for example SF44302070897. That prefix is how this site recognises a Shadowfax parcel without you choosing the courier from a list.

The number appears on the shipping label, in the seller's dispatch message, and in the SMS sent when the parcel is out for delivery.

Where to track

The official tracker is track.shadowfax.in. Enter the AWB and the page shows the current status plus the scan history. Sellers who book through Shadowfax's own dashboard see the same information in more detail, including rider assignment.

What the statuses mean

StatusMeaning
NewBooked. The AWB exists but nobody has collected the parcel yet.
Assigned for seller pickupA rider has been allocated to collect it
Out for pickupThe rider is on the way to the seller
Picked upCollected and in the network
In transitMoving between facilities
Out for deliveryWith the delivery agent
DeliveredHanded over and recorded

Why a new parcel can look stuck at "New"

"New" means the booking exists but the parcel has not been collected. For a marketplace seller that gap is usually a day; for a direct booking it depends when the pickup was scheduled. If it stays at "New" for more than two days, the pickup has probably not been assigned - and only the sender can fix that.

Returns get their own AWB

A return pickup is a separate shipment with its own number. If you are watching the original AWB while returning an item, you are watching the wrong one. The return number appears in the return request, not the original order.

When a parcel is reassigned

Shadowfax re-plans routes close to delivery, so a parcel can pass to a different delivery partner in the same city without the AWB changing. If the agent's name differs from the one in your SMS, that is why - the number stays the same throughout.

If it says delivered and is not with you

Check the reception desk, security and neighbours first. Then raise it with the seller within their claim window, which is often short. Ask them for proof of delivery - it records who signed and when.

Who actually delivers your parcel

Shadowfax operates a network of delivery partners rather than a single employed fleet in every city. The AWB stays the same while the person carrying it can change. This is normal and is not a sign that the parcel has been handed to an unrelated company.

Same-day and quick-commerce orders

Shadowfax also runs hyperlocal delivery for grocery and food apps, where the journey is measured in minutes and tracking is a live map inside the ordering app. A parcel with an SF AWB and a scan history is a normal e-commerce shipment, not a hyperlocal order - the two behave very differently and it is worth knowing which you have.

What a seller sees that you do not

The seller's dashboard shows pickup scheduling, rider assignment and failed-attempt reasons in more detail than the public tracker. If your timeline is thin, the sender can usually tell you more in one message than the public page will show.

Tracking without refreshing

Parcel status changes a handful of times a day at most. Opening the page repeatedly will not make a scan appear. Save the parcel in a tracker and check once in the morning and once in the evening - that matches how often the underlying data actually moves.

Why the public page shows less than the seller's dashboard

Shadowfax's public tracker shows status and scans; the seller's dashboard also shows pickup scheduling, rider details and the reason a delivery attempt failed. For a parcel that is stuck or was refused, the seller can see the cause in one screen while the public page shows only that an attempt happened. That is why the sender is usually the faster route to an answer.

What to send the seller if you need help

One message with the AWB, the last status you saw and the date of it gets a faster answer than a long description. The seller can see pickup scheduling and failed-attempt reasons that the public tracker does not show, so they can usually tell you in one reply what the page cannot. Keep it to those three facts and the exchange is short.
