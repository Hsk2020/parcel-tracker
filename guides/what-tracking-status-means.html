What Each Tracking Status Actually Means

Updated 9 November 2026 · Hrudai Store Kart

Courier statuses are written for internal use, not for customers. The same real-world situation is described differently by each courier, and some statuses sound more alarming than they are. Here is what they actually mean.

Before the parcel moves

Pending / Pre-advice / Manifested

The label exists. The seller has registered the shipment but the courier has not physically collected it. Do nothing. A day or two in this state is normal, and chasing the courier at this point achieves nothing because they do not have the parcel.

While it moves

In transit

Between facilities. Several of these in a row is normal, especially on long surface routes. The location shown is the hub that scanned it, not a live GPS position.

Arrived at hub / At processing centre

Sorting. The parcel is being routed to the delivery branch. Usually one to two days before an out-for-delivery scan.

The last mile

Out for delivery

With a delivery agent today. This is the only status where waiting at home genuinely matters. If nobody is available, the attempt usually fails the same day.

Delivered

Completed and recorded against a signature, OTP or a named recipient. If you did not receive it, ask the courier for the proof of delivery before assuming it was lost - it often turns out to have been accepted by a neighbour or security desk.

When something is wrong

Undelivered / Delivery failed

An attempt was made and did not succeed - nobody home, address not found, or refused. Most couriers retry the next working day, typically two or three attempts in total.

Exception

A catch-all for a problem: damaged parcel, wrong address, customs issue, or a network disruption. The status alone will not tell you which. Contact the courier with the AWB.

RTO (Return to Origin)

After failed attempts or a refusal, the parcel travels back to the seller. If you still want it, contact the seller - they usually re-dispatch once it reaches them.

On hold

Usually awaiting information or payment: an incomplete address, a customs duty demand, or an unpaid cash-on-delivery amount.

What statuses never tell you

  • Where the vehicle is. Scans are at facilities, not in transit.
  • A reliable delivery time. Estimated dates are estimates; the courier does not commit to an hour.
  • Who has it right now. Between scans, nobody can say.

The one rule worth remembering

Judge a parcel by the gap between scans, not by the status wording. Two to three days of silence mid-transit is normal; more than five working days without any scan is worth a call to the courier with your AWB.

Why two sites show two different statuses

Third-party trackers refresh on a schedule; the courier's own system is live. If one says "in transit" and the other "out for delivery", the courier is right. This is not a bug in either - it is the cost of aggregating data that is updated every few hours.

What "delivered" legally means

It means the courier recorded a handover: a signature, an OTP, or a named recipient. It does not mean the parcel reached your hands. That distinction is what makes proof of delivery the first thing to ask for, and it is usually enough to resolve a dispute in a day.
